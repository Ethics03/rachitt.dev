When I start learning something new, whether it's a concept, a research topic, or simply reading something interesting, I follow a few principles that help me retain what matters while avoiding unnecessary pressure.


Over time, I’ve also built a small system and set of tools that help me learn effectively.

            *Learn → Implement → Journal → Reflect → Iterate → Improve*

--- 

## Journaling Every day 

Firstly, journaling is the most important part of learning something and keeping it fresh. I journal every day and writing a blog of everything I learned that day.

This helps in two ways - 

1. **Reflection:**  When you write daily, you rethink the choices you made. Get better ideas and reflect upon yourself.
2. **Progress**: You clearly see what went wrong or how much you are progressing in long-term.

I mostly use an Obsidian vault for all my journals and blogs. 

---


## Organizing Learnings


When I read a research paper, the first thing I ask myself is:

 *Can I implement this?*

I note down all the important components of it in Obsidian.

Yes, I do love Obsidian but I also embrace simplicity and not going into the rabbit hole of making my vault look "cool".

It's a simple setup with each date having its own note and each note having a tag of what I learnt. 

Those tags help me retrieve the bulk of notes I wrote about a particular topic.

## Working With a Team


One habit that I have made is whenever I get an idea or implementation, I write it down in a shared notion space with my team so that everyone can read it when they need to.

This helps the team stay in sync and understanding each other's implementations and think better about them.

Using Linear for team management has been really good and I love using Linear.

Challenges :

One of the most important realizations I had was, I always wanted things to "just work" after I started implementing it. This increased pressure, over-engineering and over-thinking about the architecture.

But the truth is: **things are rarely perfect on the first attempt.** 

And that’s completely fine

Nothing is always perfect. You will always find issues, optimizations needed. But using the time effectively for maximum output is important.

---

## The Power of Iteration

Everything ultimately connects to one idea:

**Iteration**.

Life also works on iteration. You never assume you're perfect or that everything will just work after you complete a task. 

You complete a task. It works for a while. It breaks. Then you fix it. 

The key is **observing the process**.

Each iteration teaches you something about the system, and over time you need **fewer iterations** to solve similar problems.

Complete your tasks e.g, building an app, optimizing a feature in an iterative manner.

It won't be perfect. It might look messy. It might be slow. And that's okay.

Knowing when to stop over-optimizing things and making things useful is important. 

--- 

## Conclusion 

Trying to do everything the _“best possible way”_ often leads to doing everything **Mediocrely**.

Stop the unnecessary pressure. Start building things. Make them useful. 
Simplicity is what helps you build great things, because without these building blocks, nothing great exists.
