Recently, I've been trying to pay attention to how much of my day is spent thinking. Not just _what_ I think about, but how much of it is actually within my control.  
  
And from everything I've experienced so far, I've realized something uncomfortable.

  
More than half of the things we think about daily are the things that we can’t control.  
The outcome of something, the fruit of something you do or your work.  
Worrying about someone else, worrying about how they react, worrying about how someone thinks about you with every word you say.  
  
But the reality is, in the grand scheme of things, most of it you think about doesn’t matter either to others or to you. Reflecting on your actions is different from beating yourself up over them. Pity won’t help you change.  
  
What’s done is done. The actions you choose in your lifespan have consequences, either good or bad.  
  
What matters is how you react after that.  
  
After understanding myself a bit, I’ve realized it’s not that hard to pull the plug.  
All it takes is, doing something in the present.  
  
Taking 5 things that are in front of you, either it’s your favorite book, your laptop, your favorite game, or just my favorite card deck to shuffle.  
  
Living in the present, that’s all it takes to pull the plug. And when you do it you realize, more than half of those thoughts just vanish.  
  
Being present in the moment just makes your days and years way better than you might think. Because neither the regret of the past nor the worry of the future is going to change what happens.  
  
Pulling that plug is the only thing that will help you actually work on the things that you love.  
  
Planning feels really productive, because it’s about the things that “will happen” or are “going to happen”. Until you realize, all you have been doing is just planning.  
  
Planning seems productive because you think you are working on your goals, but you’re actually not.  
  
Taking action on those plans is the thing that differentiates yesterday from today.  
Doesn’t matter if it’s a good plan, a decent one or maybe just “your” plan.  
  
Being present is the only fruit you need every single day you wake up.
