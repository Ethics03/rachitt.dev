title: "the less you know, the better"
slug: "the-better"

---

The title seems self-contradictory if you see it in the sense of knowledge. But no, this title is not in the context of knowledge.

Knowing more, in the sense of gaining knowledge is always good.

The title is trying to emphasize the things that happen to us in our day-to-day lives. Events, trauma, past, etc.

After a deeper introspection on myself, I had some findings.

Most of the things that are blocking my current state of mind, either from my work or from simply enjoying the moments, are things that shouldn’t be on my mind in the first place.

The worst part is, we keep collecting the things that we know aren't going to help our current state.

Comparison is the biggest blocker of all.

Comparing your progress, your life, your duties and your outcomes to others isn’t something that is going to change anything in our lives. But yet, we still do it anyway knowing there is nothing we can do about it.

Think of the possibility if you didn't know what anyone else was struggling with or achieving, and just thought about how you would work with your own circumstances.

Wouldn't that be great? Nothing to worry about, nothing to fret about, just you and your problems to solve.

I love this verse from the book Meditations by Marcus Aurelius. It goes like this:

***“For a stone which has been thrown up, it is no evil to come down, nor any good to have been thrown up”***

A stone being thrown upward isn't inherently a victory, and coming back down isn't inherently a failure. It is simply moving according to the conditions it finds itself in.

Perhaps we do something similar with our own lives. We see someone moving “ahead” of us and immediately interpret our own position as falling behind. We see someone achieving something we haven't and turn it into evidence that we're doing something wrong.

Maybe the goal isn't to know less about the world. Maybe it is to **stop carrying information that has no use in solving the problems directly in front of us.**


Once we do that, there is nothing that is blocking us from solving the things in front of us. Abiding by the inherent nature of life and the present, accepting it, and working on what is in front of us is the source of truth for everything that will happen to us in the future, whether good or bad.


So, the conclusion I drew is. Once we are free from the cage of the information that bindings us from freely solving the things that are beneficial for our own self, nothing and especially “our own self” can stop us from progressing in the things we love to do and the things we want to become.
